TANBIH  
(ADMONITION)

بِسْمِ اللهِ الرَّحْمٰنِ الرَّحِيْمِ  
*Bismillāhir-roḥmānir-roḥīm.*  
*In the name of الله, The Most Gracious, Most Merciful.*

This Admonition is the Will of the Late SYAIKH ‘ABDULLOH MUBAROK BIN NUR MUHAMMAD PPKN 1 Roḍiyallōhu ‘Anhu, who resided in the Hermitage of Suryalaya PPKN 1 Kajembaran Rohmaniyyah. He declared to his male, female, young and old disciples, “May you all be in happiness, and الله Subḥānahu wa taʿālā pours an everlasting blissfulness and there will be no rift among us.”

May the Nation’s Leaders be increasingly honored and exalted, so that they may protect and guide all citizens in peace, justice, and prosperity—both outwardly and inwardly.

We, as a place where people come to ask about THORĪQOH QŌDIRIYYAH NAQSYABANDIYYAH MA’HAD SURYALAYA SIRNARASA PPKN, sincerely and wholeheartedly convey this advice to all students: Be cautious in all matters; do not act in ways that go against the rules of RELIGION and the STATE.

Obey both of them appropriately, such is the attitude of a person who remains steadfast in faith, manifests a willingness to submit to both RELIGION and the STATE, by adhering to the DIVINE PRESENCES (حَضْرَةِ إِلَٰهِي - ḤAḌROTU ILĀHĪ), which affirms the mandates within RELIGION and the STATE.

Reflect, O all students, and do not be swayed by the enticements of desire or influenced by the temptations of Satan. Be on guard against straying from the commands of RELIGION and the STATE, so that you may examine yourselves—lest you be drawn by the whispers of the devil, who constantly seeks to creep into the depths of our hearts.

It is better to prove the virtue that stems from sanctity:

1. Toward those who are superior to us in both outward and inward aspects, we must show respect; such is the proper conduct—to live in harmony and uphold mutual respect.
2. Toward our fellow beings who are equal to us in all aspects, we must avoid conflict and, instead, adopt a humble attitude — working together in fulfilling the commands of RELIGION and the STATE. Disputes and disagreements must be prevented, lest we fall under His words: "عَذَابٌ أَلِيمٌ - ʿadhā-bun alīm" which means a grievous torment—an eternal suffering from this world to the hereafter (physical hardship and inner sorrow).
3. Toward those whose circumstances are below our own, we must not demean or mistreat them, nor should we act arrogantly. Instead, we ought to show compassion with sincere awareness, so that they may feel comforted and joyful. They should not be made to feel fearful or alienated, as though their hearts are wounded. Rather, they should be guided and nurtured with gentle counsel that fosters awareness and leads them toward the path of virtue.
4. Toward the poor and underprivileged, we must show compassion, kindness, and courteous behavior, accompanied by generosity that reflects a conscious and empathetic heart. Let us try to place ourselves in their position—how painful it would be to live in a state of deprivation. Therefore, we must not be indifferent, seeking only our own comfort and happiness. Because their condition of poverty is not by choice, but rather a manifestation of God's will.

Such, indeed, is the attitude of a truly conscious individual, even toward strangers, for they, too, are descendants of the Prophet Adam ‘Alayhis Salām (peace be upon him). In reference to verse 70 of Suroh al-Isrō', which means: “Indeed, We honored the progeny of Adam, and bore them across land and sea and provided them with good things for their sustenance, and exalted them above many of Our creatures.”

The conclusion drawn from this verse is that we should all uphold mutual respect and avoid causing disappointment. This aligns with the message of Suroh Al-Mā'idah, which means: “Cooperate with one another in righteousness and piety, with sincere commitment to RELIGION and the STATE; and do not cooperate in sin and enmity against the commands of RELIGION and the STATE.”

As for matters of religion, they are entirely the concern of each individual according to their own faith, in light of Suroh Al-Kāfirūn, verse 6: “To you be your religion, and to me, mine.” The meaning is clear: there should be no conflict over religious differences. We are obliged to live in harmony and uphold mutual respect, but we must never interfere in one another’s religious beliefs or practices.

Reflect upon the wisdom of our ancestors: “We should conduct ourselves with virtue, order, and peace. Otherwise, regret may come too soon, and remorse afterward will be of no use,” this is because personal suffering is a consequence of one’s own actions and deeds.

In Suroh An-Nahl, verse 112, it is explained that: “الله has set forth a parable—a town, city, or nation that was once secure and at peace, prosperous and abundant in blessings. However, its inhabitants denied the favors of الله, and as a result, they were overwhelmed by calamities—famine, hardship, and fear—brought upon them by their own actions and behavior.”

Therefore, all students should act with careful consideration in every path they take, for the sake of both outward and inward well-being, in this world and the hereafter, so that the heart remains at peace and the body stays in comfort. Under no circumstances should conflict arise, as the ultimate goal is "Noble Character and Physical Perfection" (Cageur Bageur).

Our practice of THORĪQOH QŌDIRIYYAH NAQSYABANDIYYAH MA’HAD SURYALAYA SIRNARASA PPKN must be carried out with the utmost sincerity and dedication, in order to attain all forms of virtue and to avoid all forms of evil, both outward and inward, concerning the physical and spiritual aspects of the self, which are constantly surrounded by the temptations of the ego and the deceit of Satan.

This Will must be carried out diligently by all students in order to attain salvation in both this world and the Hereafter.

HERMITAGE OF SURYALAYA, 13 February 1956.  
This Will is hereby addressed to all fellow brethren,  
Signed,  
*(SYAIKH AHMAD SHOHIBUL WAFA TAJUL ’ARIFIN, PPKN 2, ROḌIYALLŌHU ʿANHU, QODDASALLŌHU SIRROH(U))*

STRINGS OF PEARLS (Words of Wisdom):
1. DO NOT HARBOR HATRED TOWARD CONTEMPORARY SCHOLARS;
2. DO NOT CRITICIZE THE TEACHINGS OF OTHERS;
3. DO NOT SCRUTINIZE THE STUDENTS OF OTHERS;
4. DO NOT CHANGE YOUR ATTITUDE, EVEN IF YOU ARE MISTREATED;
5. MUST BE COMPASSIONATE TO THOSE WHO HATE YOU.

*(The Late Revered Teacher)*
